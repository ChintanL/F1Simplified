What is Formula 1, in one paragraph?

Teams build the fastest cars the rules allow, and two drivers per team race them around the world on about 24 weekends a year. There are two championships: one for drivers and one for teams (called constructors). Cars are single-seaters with open wheels, and they can reach over 340 km/h.

What happens on a race weekend?

Friday is practice: teams test the car. Saturday has qualifying, a time-trial session that decides who starts at the front. Sunday is the race, usually about 300 km. Some weekends swap in a shorter “sprint” race on Saturday for extra points.

How do points work?

The top ten finishers score in the Grand Prix: 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1. A sprint race pays the top eight: 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. A team’s score is its two drivers’ points added together.

Why does starting position matter so much?

Cars follow each other closely, and the car behind loses grip in the “dirty air” of the car ahead. That makes passing hard, so a good qualifying lap often decides the race. The green and red numbers in the results below show how many places a driver gained or lost from the start.

Why do they stop in the middle of the race?

Pit stops. Tyres wear out, and a crew changes all four in about two seconds. Teams pick softer tyres (fast, wear quickly) or harder ones (slower, last longer), and the race is a puzzle of when to stop.

What is a safety car?

If there is a crash or debris, a safety car leads the pack at slow speed while marshals clear the track. It bunches everyone up, wipes out big leads, and often shakes up the result.

What do the flags mean?

Marshals use flags to talk to drivers.

Green: track is clearYellow: danger, slow down, no passingRed: race stoppedBlue: faster car is behind, let it byChequered: race is over
What are the cars doing when they pass?

Drivers get help on the straights, such as extra electric power or flaps that cut drag. The exact system changes with the rules, and the 2026 cars use new power units and active aerodynamics, so the details are different from a few years ago. The idea is the same: make passing possible without making it easy.

What is a penalty?

Stewards punish rule-breaking. Common ones are time added to a driver’s race time, losing grid places, or a drive-through through the pit lane. A driver can finish first on the road and still lose the win if a penalty drops them down the order.
